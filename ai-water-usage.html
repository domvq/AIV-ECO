How much water does AI use?

Every AI answer runs on servers in a data center. Those servers use electricity and get hot, and both cooling them and generating their electricity can consume fresh water. Here's what's known about AI water usage, why the numbers vary so much, and what you can do about it.

Where AI's water goes

Cooling data centers

Many data centers cool servers with water that evaporates. This is the water used on site.

Generating electricity

Power plants that supply data centers also consume water. Researchers count this as off-site water, and it is often the larger share.

Making the hardware

Manufacturing chips and servers uses water too, though it's usually counted separately from running the AI.

How much water does one AI prompt use?

Estimates vary widely. In 2025, Google reported that its median Gemini Apps text prompt used about 0.24 watt-hours of energy and 0.26 milliliters of water, about five drops. Earlier research from UC Riverside, Making AI Less "Thirsty", estimated that GPT-3 consumed a 500 mL bottle of water for roughly 10 to 50 medium-length responses, depending on when and where it ran.

The figures differ because they measure different models, data centers, and boundaries: some count only on-site cooling, others include the water used to generate electricity. What all of them agree on is that more computing means more energy and more water, and that long answers, large models, image and video generation, and repeated web searches all add up.

Ways to reduce AI water usage

Skip AI for simple things

A calculator or unit converter doesn't need a data center.

Don't ask twice

Reuse good answers instead of asking the same question again.

Use a smaller model

Most everyday questions don't need the largest AI model.

Keep it short

Shorter prompts and answers mean less computing.

Search less, read smarter

Reading a few pages as plain text uses far less than fully loading many pages.

Questions

Why does AI use water?

AI runs on servers that need electricity and cooling. Data centers often use evaporating water for cooling, and power plants consume water to generate electricity.

Is AI water usage a problem?

One prompt uses a small amount, but billions of prompts add up, and some data centers sit in areas where fresh water is scarce. Researchers have called for more transparency about AI's water footprint.
